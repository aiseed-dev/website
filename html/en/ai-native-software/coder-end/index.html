AI now does the
software engineer's work.

The coder obviously goes — and the software engineer who designs and codes becomes, not a designer-coder, but someone who works in dialogue with AI (the builder)

2026.06.15 🌐 日本語

The coder who writes code obviously and completely disappears. But the subject of this chapter is what lies beyond. AI now does the work of the software engineer who designs, too.

1-02 showed that both maintenance and development become work done in dialogue with AI. This chapter takes up the other face of that, the role itself. The claim is not "all programmers disappear." It is that the role definitions called coder and software engineer disappear. That distinction is half of the argument.

The coder and the software engineer are different roles

This sub-series distinguishes two roles.

  • Coder — writing code itself is the center of the work. Requirements and design both arrive from someone else. The yardstick is "writes fast, correctly, readably."
  • Software engineer, or SE — goes into design. Decides the structure of what to build and how, and then writes the code. Broader than the coder.

Both are definitions of a role, not labels for people. The same person can work as a coder in one situation and an SE in another. What disappears is the role, not the people.

These roles were viable because writing code and designing took humans a lot of time. Shaping even a single system demanded an enormous number of person-hours, and you had to assemble a workforce both to write and to design. In Japan, the SIer industry, contract development, and multi-tier subcontracting are all built on that premise. 3-03 takes it up.

AI becomes both the coder and the software engineer

1-01 established that AI became the strongest SIer. It writes code, and it understands context and designs. The ability has range.

  • Opus — a first-rate coder. Hand it intent and it translates that into running code
  • Fable / Mythos — software engineers. They understand context, go into design, and decide structure themselves

So AI does the coder's work and the software engineer's work alike. The coder, who only writes code, obviously disappears. But the SE, who designs too, is in the same position. If AI does both the design and the code, there is no longer a place for a human to stand in the role of designing and writing the code themselves. *The market value of the design-and-code band converges to near zero.* This is not a statement about labor ethics. It is a statement about prices.

What stays with humans is dialogue with AI

So what stays with humans? Not design and not coding. It is building and operating a system in dialogue with AI.

  • Deciding what to build — the context is held by the human
  • Procuring hardware, which is the physical world
  • Negotiating with stakeholders, which is the social world
  • Running it and fixing it, continuously, which is operations and maintenance
  • Deciding direction and taking responsibility

AI processes context when it is given, and it designs. But deciding what to count as context, and what to reconcile with reality, stays with humans. So does taking responsibility. Under current institutions, the subject that holds that responsibility is not the AI. This is not the software engineer's work of designing and writing the code yourself. It is shaping things in dialogue with AI, which is a different role. 1-04 names it the builder.

What stays with humans is not design and not coding. It is building and operating a system in dialogue with AI. That is no longer the software engineer, but the builder.

What goes away is designing and writing the code yourself

So what goes away is the role of designing and writing the code yourself, the coder and the software engineer, together with the division of labor the SIer built to mass-produce it. Demand does not vanish. Both design and code get replaced by AI, so no price holds. One person, in dialogue with AI, builds and operates the system, and moves into that role. 1-04 names it the builder.

In both diagrams the customer stands in the same place. What changed is that the customer who used to only place the order now stands on the side that builds and runs it. Even just commissioning an SIer used to take real effort: writing the RFP, selecting the vendor, reconciling requirements, negotiating the contract. At the level of a design-capable AI, Fable or Mythos, that same effort of ordering is enough for the customer to build the thing outright. 1-05 takes this up.

Once, even just placing the order with an SIer took real effort. Now, that same effort is enough, and the customer builds it.

This is not "every programmer loses their job." People who have been called programmers split in two directions.

  • (a) Leave software development — move to a different industry or role
  • (b) Move to the builder — stand on the side that builds and runs a system in dialogue with AI, defined in 1-04

Conversely, builders are not drawn only from programmers. People at the field, those who actually know the work and the customer, can become builders too. What a builder needs is not the ability to write code. It is the ability to grasp the field's context and shape it in dialogue with AI. If anything, the field people, who already hold the context, are closer to the builder. 1-05 takes this up as "the customer builds it themselves."

History has the same shape. In Japan in the 1970s, calculators erased the skill of commercial calculation by abacus, the soroban. But people who could read what the numbers meant and keep the work running stayed on, in accounting and finance. The same happened with the human computer in the West, and with the typesetter when phototypesetting replaced letterpress. When handwork is replaced by machines, what splits is who can move to the broader side, meaning orchestration, dialogue, operations, and responsibility, and who cannot. The same thing is happening now in software development, across both coding and design.

The thing to flag is the speed. After the Casio Mini (1972, ¥12,800) and other low-priced models arrived, calculators pushed the abacus out of Japanese offices and homes within roughly a decade. The intuition that this kind of change takes decades only looks slow in hindsight. For the people inside it, it is fast. The AI shift is starting from a price structure orders of magnitude lower (1-01). It is reasonable to expect the same speed or faster. Whether one can absorb it becomes a question of industry structure, not personal choice. 3-06 takes that up.

Summary

AI carries both design and code. Meanwhile, deciding what to build, hardware, people, operations, dialogue, and responsibility stay with humans. Who carries that role?

And the foundational discipline of that role shifts from software engineering to the liberal arts. That is the bass line of this sub-series.

The next chapter defines that role: the builder.
