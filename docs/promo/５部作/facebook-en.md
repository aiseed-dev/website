Last week I published five pieces on AI's economy and geopolitics. The last one argued that the new manufacturing is self-sufficient in software, earns in the physical, and defends itself.

Then I noticed the hole in my own conclusion.

Part five said the AI revolution drove the cost of software toward zero — so build it yourself. But I left something out. **Attacking is software too.** One change made both building and attacking cheap, and I had written up only one side.

There is a way out, though: **verifying is software as well.** Attacking held the advantage because the attacker needs one hole while the defender must close all of them — but that came from the defender running out of hands, not from a difference in capability. Remove the labor constraint and the asymmetry goes. The end state is an equilibrium where attack and defense are both cheap and roughly equal.

What should worry you is not the equilibrium. It's that some parties cannot move to it.

Compatibility was Microsoft's strength — decades-old software still runs. Leave it and it can't withstand AI-driven attack; break it and customers leave. What was promised protection is what prevents protection.
