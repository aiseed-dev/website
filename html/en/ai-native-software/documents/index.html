Keep the text as text,
open the forms yourself.

Keep the content as text in git. Word and Claude Docs become doors you pass through, not the place the content lives

2026.07.04 🌐 日本語

What you take back in documents is not the storage. It is how you hold the manuscript.

Inside the gate (2-04), the first tool to place is documents. But the conclusion of this chapter is not "which editor to stand up." As of 2026, a document has three places it can live: Office, your own stack, and Claude Docs. All three are convenient, and in all three the key leans toward either someone else or you. So the thing to decide first is what form the manuscript is held in.

Office is something you pass through, not something you work inside

Do not misread the reason for stepping away from Office. This is not about efficiency. Thirty minutes becoming thirty seconds happens as a side effect; it is not the point.

Split clerical work into three.

  • The entrance — files that arrive from other people (Word, Excel, PDF)
  • The middle — where you think, work, and save
  • The exit — files you hand to other people (Word, Excel, PDF)

Most people have done all three inside Office. A Word file arrives, you open it in Word, edit it as Word, and send it back as Word. But as long as the middle is also Office, AI never becomes a colleague — the next section shows why.

So treat .docx, .xlsx, and .pptx not as where the content lives, but as exchange formats for dealing with people at the entrance and the exit. Do not change the organization's rules. Take back control of your own middle, and nothing else.

Inside Office, AI never becomes a colleague

Why move the content out of a closed format? Because *as long as you are inside Office, AI stays a tool and never becomes a colleague.*

Hand a Word file to AI and a conversion happens every time. Unzip the .docx, read the XML, strip the formatting, pull out the text. Excel is the same: cell coordinates, formatting, merged cells, and cross-sheet references sit between the AI and the content.

The result is that AI is usable but not a colleague. Ask it to read the whole thing and organize the issues, and the layout breaks. Ask it to analyze a table, and merged cells and formatted values confuse it.

The moment the content drops down to text and structured data, that wall disappears. The AI reads directly, writes directly, and gives back judgment. Office survives as an editing tool. The content, though, stays somewhere the AI can touch.

From the person who processes to the person who decides

As long as you do clerical work inside Office, you remain the person who processes: the one who totals the spreadsheet, tidies the document, re-pastes the numbers. That work can be replaced by AI, and as AI gets cheaper, organizations pull people out of the role.

Bring the content down to structure and the role changes. You become the person who works out what should be done, decides how to read it, designs the new mechanism, and decides what to ask the AI for. Not the amount of work but the substance of the work changes.

AI replaces the person who processes. It cannot replace the person who decides. Taking back the content means moving to the side that is not replaced.

A worked example: the monthly report — same report, different job

Take the monthly sales report.

The old flow. Open the sales data in Excel. Pivot it. Build the chart. Paste it into Word. Write the prose. Export a PDF and send it to your manager. The job you are doing here is tidying numbers.

The new flow. Read the underlying data; an arriving .xlsx is ingested by machine. Have the AI write the aggregation and produce a table. Embed the chart. Let the AI draft the prose. You add the interpretation and the judgment. Convert to .docx or PDF at the exit. The job you are doing here is working out what the numbers mean.

"This month is up 12% on last month — which customer drove it? Does it continue? Should the sales strategy change?" Those questions never surfaced while you were running pivot operations. With structured data in hand and an AI beside you, they come up on their own.

Hold the manuscript as text, and commit only the manuscript

This is the center of the chapter. There are only two decisions.

First, hold the manuscript as AsciiDoc (.adoc) text. It is a text file, so git gives you a diff. Who changed which line, and when, is on the record. A .docx is compressed XML, so its diff is unreadable. Held as text, the AI reads it and writes it directly.

Second, commit only the manuscript; keep the design out of git. The template that decides the appearance lives somewhere other than the repository. Apply a different template to the same manuscript and it comes out in a different form. What remains in git history is only the diff of what was said.

manuscripts/      ← in git. only .adoc text
  2026-09-report.adoc
templates/        ← not in git. appearance
  report.tmpl.adoc
output/           ← not in git. what was printed
  2026-09-report.pdf

Everything downstream gets easier. If the manuscript is text, you choose the exit: print a PDF, export a .docx for someone else, render HTML for the site. All of them come out of the same manuscript.

And exchanging data as AsciiDoc is going to matter. Between people, and between people and AI, once the thing being exchanged is text, nothing is lost in transit.

Form-fidelity work belongs to aiseed office — ONLYOFFICE does not hold Japanese forms

Manuscripts can be text. Forms cannot. Take a .docx application form, open it at the prescribed page count, with the prescribed line positions and column widths, correct it, and print it. In that job, format compatibility is the content.

For that job, ONLYOFFICE does not hold up on Japanese forms. That is why aiseed office (aiseed-dev/officework) is being built: an engine that reads and writes .docx and .xlsx and typesets straight to PDF.

pip install officework

As checked on 2026-09-20, the engine is at 0.5.0 on PyPI, published 2026-09-04. The beta label is off. The published release notes carry a line-by-line comparison against PDFs that Japanese government offices publish.

Document Mean line-end deviation Lines within 1pt
Survey form (110 lines) 9.01pt → 2.50pt 5 → 68
Notice (141 lines) 3.71pt → 2.54pt 5 → 83

Write down what has not arrived, too. *Only the engine is distributed; the GUI application is not yet.* Some documents wrap one character off because of how table column widths are taken. Some spreadsheets paginate differently from the original. And reading and writing AsciiDoc is not shipped yet. The decision in the previous section is a direction; this tool does not do all of it today.

A document now has three places to live — where to put Claude Docs

On 2026-09-16, Claude Docs shipped in beta. Creating a document in conversation, editing it directly, commenting on it together, and sharing it all happen inside Claude. As checked on 2026-09-20 it is available on Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise.

It is convenient. Nothing to install. As a place to draft, revise, and talk something through, it takes fewer steps than anything else available.

At the same time, the structure is the same as Microsoft 365's.

  • The document itself lives inside claude.ai. *You cannot go and fetch it from outside.* Only Claude's own tools can read it
  • Opening a shared link requires the other person to have a Claude account
  • Team and Enterprise documents cannot yet be shared outside the organization
  • Organizations using customer-managed encryption keys, zero data retention, or a HIPAA-ready configuration cannot use it yet

One thing is different. There is a clear exit. It exports to Word, PDF, Markdown, and Google Docs. Exporting as far as Markdown means the manuscript can come down to your own disk as text.

So its place is settled. *Use Claude Docs as the place to draft and circulate a manuscript. Export the finished manuscript as Markdown, convert it to AsciiDoc, and commit it.* The decision in the previous section then holds: the content stays with you, and Claude Docs becomes a door you pass through.

Settle how to compare the cost here, too. The comparison is not against a free tool. It is against what you already pay a vendor. If a production company writes the copy for your public site, compare against that invoice. If you pay per-seat licenses every month, compare against that total. As of 2026-09-20, Claude Pro is $20 a month.

A document now has three places to live. The question is not which to pick. Hold the manuscript as text and all three become doors you pass through.

Why not add another storage application

"Documents" tempts you to stand up a whole OneDrive replacement like Nextcloud. But Nextcloud is a heavy PHP monolith that drags in its own user system and its own database. It points the opposite way from the single-binary lightness this part has chosen throughout.

In 2-04 you already stood up PocketBase, which holds authentication. For document storage, make that the gate and files are enough for the rest.

  • The thing itself — the file. No heavy storage application
  • Authentication — 2-04's gate works as-is. Do not create a second login
  • Permissions and sharing — held by a local structured store, with the gate confirming identity

The files stay files — the gate holds identity, a store holds permissions

Keep documents as plain files on your own storage. Do not embed them as blobs in records. Keep .adoc, .docx, and .xlsx as files, because then machines (Polars, AI) can read them directly later.

The first choice is where permissions live.

  • A structured store (recommended) — if PostgreSQL is already up, put access control there. For a small setup with only the gate, the gate's SQLite is enough. It can be queried, transactions apply, and pg_dump preserves it
  • xattr (minimum dependencies) — if you refuse to add any store, pin them to the file's extended attributes. But xattrs are silently dropped by cp, rsync, tar, and backups. Confirm that attributes survive every copy and backup path
-- access control in a structured store: queryable, and it does not vanish
-- doc_acl(doc_id, subject, perm); subject is 'alice' or 'team:sales'
SELECT doc_id FROM doc_acl WHERE subject IN ('alice', 'team:sales') AND perm >= 'r';

You do not need a heavy separate application just for permissions. The gate holds identity; the structured store holds permissions.

Moving existing documents

Documents piled up in OneDrive and SharePoint move without a format change.

# pull them out with rclone and lay them on your own storage
rclone copy onedrive:Documents ./inbox --progress
# record each file's permissions into the structured store (one script run)

Migrate in stages. Run both sides in parallel, and cancel the old storage only after the move is finished (2-10).

Write everything new as .adoc from the start. You do not have to convert the back catalog. *Start with new manuscripts as text, and the storage turns over on its own.*

Dealing with the outside — hand it over at the exit

You do not need a dedicated sharing platform for customers and suppliers. What goes outside goes out through the exit: not the source file, but a .docx or a PDF, sent by mail (2-07). Receiving is the same — a file that arrives is opened at the entrance and then brought in.

Do not create gate accounts for outsiders. Parties who genuinely need to co-edit are rare; exchanging versions is almost always enough. Send it, it comes back corrected. The way it has worked since paper.

People use the editor, machines use Polars

This is where 2-03 pays off. *People enter and read through an editing tool; machines handle the underlying data with Polars and DuckDB.* The same .xlsx is an editing tool to a person and an input to a machine.

  • Summary sheets, application forms, proposals a person writes — opened and stored in aiseed office
  • Millions of rows reconciled, company-wide totals — Polars reads the .xlsx and writes PostgreSQL

The spreadsheet goes back to being a human tool, and the heavy processing moves to the machine's tools.

A reference implementation — kura

This arrangement is actually built in the public repository kura (aiseed-dev/workspace), a self-hosted alternative to Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace aimed at small and mid-sized business use.

  • Authentication — PocketBase (replaceable token verification with a short cache)
  • Permissions — minimum dependencies, held in the file's xattrs
  • The thing itself — the file
  • Editing — an editing engine layered on with JWT and a callback (FastAPI + Flet)

How to check you are done

This chapter is done when these five hold.

  1. Your own .docx form opens in aiseed office and prints at the same page count as Word
  2. An .adoc manuscript committed to git shows only the lines you changed in the diff
  3. Both a PDF and a .docx come out of the same manuscript
  4. A person logged in through the gate sees only the documents they have rights to
  5. Copies made with cp -a and rsync -X still carry the permissions (if you use xattrs)
pip install officework
git diff manuscripts/2026-09-report.adoc   # only the changed lines
getfattr -d document.docx                  # only if you use xattrs

What the human holds

Values the human supplies

  • The path of the document store
  • The signing key shared between the editing engine and the gate
  • The rule for who can open which documents (departments, roles)

Actions the AI states before performing

  • Converting or moving existing documents in bulk
  • Rewriting the access-control table
  • Sending a file outside the company

Versions checked, and when

  • aiseed office (officework) engine 0.5.0. pip install officework. Published 2026-09-04, confirmed on PyPI on 2026-09-20
  • The GUI application and AsciiDoc read/write are not shipped as of 2026-09-20
  • Claude Docs shipped in beta on 2026-09-16, available on Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise. Checked 2026-09-20
  • Permissions live in PostgreSQL or the gate's SQLite
  • This procedure was written on 2026-09-20
  • If a version has moved, have the AI confirm the official procedure before proceeding

Summary

Do not pick a storage place. Decide how the manuscript is held.

  • Manuscript as AsciiDoc text — git gives a diff; the AI reads and writes it directly
  • Commit only the manuscript — design lives elsewhere, so history carries only substance
  • Forms belong to aiseed office — built because ONLYOFFICE does not hold Japanese forms. Engine at 0.5.0; the GUI and AsciiDoc support are not there yet
  • Claude Docs is a door — drafting and co-editing are fast; export the finished manuscript as Markdown and bring it home
  • The files stay files — gate for identity, structured store for permissions, no heavy storage app
  • The comparison is not against free — compare against what you already pay a vendor
